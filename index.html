Stops

Camp: 6 hours or more. Snack/rest: 1 to 6 hours, and a snack/rest break after each spell of rowing (set above). Stops of an hour or more (waits at tidal gates too) go to the past campsite that costs the least extra rowing, rowing back up to 4 nm if none is ahead; when the day's rowing hours run out the stop is made a camp there. A few short pauses stay on the water where no past campsite is in reach. Click a row to set the map time.

Storm contingency

Runs the plan from the planner page many times, each with storms dropped in at random. When a storm warning comes the crew makes for the nearest past campsite or beach and stays ashore until it has blown through; then the plan carries on under the same rules, so the tides, gates and camps all move. Stage 2 only (Victoria to Ketchikan).

What the storms cost

Days lost against the storm days. A storm can cost more than its own length when the tides and gates no longer line up afterwards, or less when it falls on a stop you were making anyway.

Storm shelters

Where crews sat out the storms, most used first. Cumulative share: how many of all storm stops the list covers down to that row, so a short list can cover most of them. Click a row to see it on the map.

Gaps: no shelter in reach

Stretches where a storm caught the crew with no past campsite or beach within reach, so it was sat out on the water. Worth scouting for a landing, or rowing through only with a good forecast.
